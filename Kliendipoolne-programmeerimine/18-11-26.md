A student who had already finished got a bigger practice task (posted in the meeting chat):

### 3. 💶 Free software, paid licences

Some frameworks have parts that cost money, and a question about that led to a longer talk
about why that is fair:

- **For a company the price is small.** With even five employees on an average Estonian
  salary, wages are several thousand euros every month, so a licence of a few hundred euros
  is hardly noticeable. Things are priced differently for companies anyway: an office
  internet connection costs a lot more than the same connection at home.
- **Free things are made by people.** Building, hosting and maintaining a library takes a lot
  of work. It is odd when the richest companies use it for free while the author cannot even
  get a coffee out of it.
- **Unpaid projects die.** Plenty of useful projects have disappeared or broken because the
  one person keeping them alive for free finally stopped. Open source works at scale where
  money flows back, for example Linux, where companies sell services around it and pass part
  of the income on to the people working on it.
- **Licences also give information**: who uses the software, how much and for what, which
  helps the authors set priorities.

For students, volunteers and hobby projects the free versions are there; a company making a
profit can afford to pay.
